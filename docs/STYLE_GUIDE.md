# Teaching-prose style guide

Every note, concept card, comment reply and proposal in Mains Atlas follows this guide, so
the whole app reads in one voice: a good teacher's revision note, in sentences you could
write straight into a Mains answer.

The user approved this register on 2026-10-16. The two before/after pairs they approved are
kept with the private notes (`NOTE_TEMPLATE.md` in the notes repo). The examples below use
other topics.

## Voice

- **Plain and direct.** Write the way a strong candidate writes an answer, not the way a
  briefing memo is written.
- **One idea per sentence.** Aim for 20 words or fewer. Where a sentence turns on "but",
  "so" or "because", it can usually become two sentences.
- **Subject first, active verbs.** "The Supreme Court struck down the law", not "The law
  was struck down by the Court".
- **Full name first, short name after.** "The Women's Reservation Act (106th Amendment)",
  then "the Act". "Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs)", then "PRIs".
- **Say the "so what" in plain words.** End a point with the takeaway: "The bottleneck is
  parties, not voters."

## Avoid

These make text read as machine-written:

- **Colon chains and arrows in sentences.** "X: Y → Z" belongs in a flowchart, not prose.
  Use colons only after a bold label (**Political:**) or before a list.
- **Semicolons joining two ideas.** Use two sentences.
- **Abstract jargon.** For example: front, trigger, bites, puzzle, missing link, supply
  problem, landscape, leverage, robust, crucial, pivotal, delve, nuanced, multifaceted,
  "plays a key role", "in a nutshell", "a double-edged sword".
- **Filler hedges used as tics.** For example: arguably, notably, importantly, interestingly,
  "it is worth noting".
- **Lines about the note itself.** "This section holds 8 points" is a drafting rule. Rules
  belong in the template.
- **Rhetorical questions, exclamation marks, and openers** like "In today's world".
- **Em dashes for asides.** Use a full stop or a comma.

## Analysis and opinion

- **Name who holds a view.** "The Second Administrative Reforms Commission recommended…",
  "PRS points out…", "Opposition parties argue…".
- **State your own reading plainly.** Put it as a closing takeaway, without "arguably":
  "So reserved seats are unlikely before the 2029 election." Use "likely" or "may" only
  when the uncertainty is real.
- **Never present a view as a court's holding.** What the Court held and what commentators
  say stay separate.

## Facts, numbers and dates

- **Dates.** "16 April 2026" in prose, "Apr 2026" in tables. Never "recently"; write
  "as of Oct 2026".
- **Money and large counts in the Indian system.** "8 lakh women", "₹1.2 lakh crore".
- **Percentages as the source gives them.** "46.6%", not "nearly half", unless rounding
  is the point.
- **Articles.** "Article 15(3)" the first time in prose; "Art 15(3)" after that and in tables.
- **Cases.** Name and year: "Kihoto Hollohan (1992)". Use the full title only on its card.
- **Spelling.** Indian English with British spelling (organisation, programme, labour).
  Keep "judgment" without the middle "e", as Indian courts do.

## Sources

- **Never put a source inside a sentence.** No "(Scroll)" or "according to LiveLaw" mid-line.
- **Every fact still has a source,** as a citation marker after the sentence or bullet. The
  app shows these as small numbered markers that reveal the source on tap, with a Sources
  list at the end of the page. In the Markdown copy a marker is ` ([Label](url))` at the end.
- **Keep labels short:** "PRS", "Gazette", "SC judgment", "PIB", "The Hindu, 14 Oct 2026".

## Structure

- **Each section opens with one plain sentence** saying where things stand.
- **One fact or claim per bullet.** Bold a lead word only when it helps scanning
  (**For:**, **Concerns:**).
- **Tables only for real comparisons:** provision and what it does; case, what it held and
  how to use it; then and now. Keep cells short. If a cell needs two sentences, use bullets.
- **Use UPSC's own words for headings:** Significance, Challenges, Concerns, Way forward.
- **Flowcharts** carry the arrows. Label each step with a short phrase and its date.

## Before and after

> **Before:** 73rd Amendment: one-third quota → women's entry, but proxy rule persists:
> "sarpanch pati" undercuts real power.
>
> **After:** The 73rd Amendment (1992) reserved one-third of panchayat seats for women. Many
> more women now hold office. But in many villages a male relative still takes the
> decisions, the so-called "sarpanch pati".

> **Before:** Finance Commission advice: non-binding yet rarely overturned, arguably a
> convention-backed constraint on the Union.
>
> **After:** The Finance Commission's advice is not binding. Still, the Union has usually
> accepted its main recommendations, so in practice they carry real weight.
